# Human Voice Copy Guide — Xoxoday (All Products)

Generalized from the Empuls playbook. Use this voice for **Empuls, Plum, Compass, Loyalife, and Partnerships** outbound copy.

---

## 1. THE CORE PRINCIPLE

Write like a genuinely curious peer, not a vendor. Every email/message should read like it could have been typed by a human rep in two minutes, not templated by marketing. If a line sounds like it belongs in a brochure, cut it.

**The tell you're checking for:** Could this line survive being read out loud to the prospect on a call? If it sounds like copy, rewrite it.

---

## 2. SEQUENCE ARCHITECTURE (5-email arc)

| # | Job | Opening move | Closing move |
|---|-----|--------------|---------------|
| **1** | Name a specific, narrow pain tied to this exact persona/vertical/product. No pitch yet. | Casual pattern-interrupt question or observation | Soft, low-stakes CTA ("Worth a reply either way?") |
| **2** | Introduce product by name, inline, almost as an aside. First proof point (named customer + one concrete detail/number). | "Following up on X." | Direct but easy CTA ("Want me to walk you through it?") |
| **3** | Reframe the solution around what THIS persona actually cares about (their KPI, their pain, their goal). Second proof point or mechanic. | "Here's the kind/reason/thought..." | An either/or diagnostic question, not a pitch question |
| **4** | The "big proof" beat — board-level stakes, G2 rating, or a leadership-visible outcome. | "Last thought" / restates the stakes | Ask for 15 minutes, framed as "I'd rather show you X than keep describing it" |
| **5** | The breakup / permission-to-leave email. No new pitch. | "I'll leave you alone on this one" / "I'll stop circling back" | Door left open, zero pressure ("Just reply, easy to pick back up") |

**LinkedIn mirrors this same 5-beat arc** (M1–M5) but:
- Shorter, chattier, more fragments
- Never reuses a line or stat verbatim from the email track
- M1 = new-connection opener; M2 = "been a while" re-engagement; M3–M4 = value/proof; M5 = soft exit

---

## 3. SENTENCE-LEVEL RULES

**Do:**
- Short sentences. Vary rhythm — some are one clause, some run a bit longer, but nothing baroque.
- Contractions everywhere: "it's," "don't," "I'll," "you're."
- Sentence fragments for punch: *"Fine, until someone upstairs asks for one number and there isn't one to give."*
- Concrete, sensory specifics over abstractions: *"a teller rings up a referral at 3pm,"* *"a sales rep finally sees their commission live before month-end,"* not "team members" or "people."
- Named customer proof with ONE crisp detail attached (a timeframe, a %, a rating) — never a generic case-study dump. E.g., *"Nielsen had recognition live across the whole company in 45 days."* or *"Freshworks cut their reward-delivery overhead by 90%."*
- Rhetorical questions that are genuinely diagnostic, not manipulative: *"Is that familiar, or have you found a way around it?"*
- Self-aware, low-ego asides: *"Quick one, and ignore me if it's not a thing for you."* / *"I'll stop circling back."*
- End most emails with a question, not a statement.

**Don't:**
- No em dashes.
- No buzzwords: never "revolutionize," "seamless," "unlock," "leverage," "solution," "empower," "game-changer," "transformative."
- No exclamation points.
- No feature dumps. One mechanic per email, max.
- No hard-sell CTAs ("Book a demo now"). CTAs stay soft, phrased as a favor or an easy yes ("Worth a reply either way?" "Got 15 minutes?").
- Never repeat a line, stat, or proof point across the email and LinkedIn tracks for the same persona.
- Don't over-explain the product. Say what it does in one clause, then move back to the prospect's world.

## 4. PRODUCT-SPECIFIC TONE OVERLAY

### Empuls (Employee Recognition + Engagement)
**Pain framing:** Recognition is fragmented (Slack shoutouts, spreadsheets, annual award nights). It doesn't scale, doesn't integrate, and leaders can't prove ROI.
**Proof style:** Named customer + adoption timeline. E.g., "Bosch rolled it out to 8,000 people in 60 days."
**Seniority mapping:** HR/People Ops (VP) → QBR reporting, budget justification. Director → tool sprawl, adoption friction. C-suite → board optics, retention as strategic.

### Plum (Rewards API + Global Catalog)
**Pain framing:** Manual rewards management. Gift cards from procurement (slow), single-country catalogs (limited), or no choice at all (low redemption).
**Proof style:** Named customer + outcome metric. E.g., "Borderless Access reduced delivery time by 90% and saved 16% on costs."
**Seniority mapping:** RevOps/Marketing (VP) → cost savings, integration simplicity. Director → speed/friction (30 seconds to redeem). C-suite → global expansion, zero ops overhead.

### Compass (Sales Commission Automation)
**Pain framing:** Commission spreadsheets. Manual calcs, month-end disputes, reps can't see real-time earnings, finance recalculates by hand.
**Proof style:** Named customer + accuracy/time gain. E.g., "A 400-rep SaaS team hit 95% first-calc accuracy and reclaimed 3 FTE-weeks per month."
**Seniority mapping:** Sales Ops/RevOps (VP) → ROI upward, dispute reduction, live payout visibility. Director → rep adoption, dispute tickets. C-suite → finance headcount recovery, rep satisfaction.

### Loyalife (Customer Loyalty Programs)
**Pain framing:** Loyalty is generic (points + tier system). Low engagement, churn on redemption, no brand differentiation.
**Proof style:** Named customer + engagement/retention lift. E.g., "A wellness brand cut churn by 12% and tripled redemption rates after launching Loyalife."
**Seniority mapping:** CX/Growth (VP) → churn reduction, CLTV impact. Director → activation/engagement metrics, tier migration. C-suite → brand loyalty as strategic differentiator.

### Partnerships (Co-sell with SAP, UKG, AWS, Salesforce, etc.)
**Pain framing:** Integration headaches. Partners see a gap in their stack; end-customers don't know Xoxoday exists; deals get gated on "does it integrate?"
**Proof style:** Named co-customer + joint outcome. E.g., "A UKG customer deployed Empuls + Compass in 8 weeks across 15 countries."
**Seniority mapping:** Partner GTM / Sales Leader → deal acceleration, joint pipeline. Enterprise Account Manager → customer stickiness. Partner Executive → market expansion, revenue share.

## 5. PERSONA-LEVEL FRAMING (what changes by seniority)

| Seniority | What they actually care about | Proof style to use | Sample pain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C-suite / Board-level** | Strategic narrative, board optics, vendor scrutiny, long-term ROI | G2 rating, "the board buys a chart, not a story," named enterprise customer, headcount/budget recovery | "We can't prove this drives retention to the board" |
| **VP / Director** | Proving ROI upward, QBR reporting pain, reducing manual work, adoption friction | Time saved, cost saved, adoption jumps, named-customer timeline, "chart already drawn" | "We spend 20% of ops time on manual X" |
| **Individual Contributor** | Day-to-day friction, tool sprawl, time-to-action, visibility | Speed/friction stats ("30 seconds from a phone"), real-time dashboards, one-click actions | "I can't see my real number until the 3rd" |

## 6. VERTICAL-SPECIFIC PAIN (Empuls/Engagement angle)

Always ground pain in a *scene*, not an abstraction:

- **Banking / Financial Services:** Branch-level recognition gap, teller attrition, producer retention invisible to HQ
- **Healthcare / Pharma:** Night/weekend shifts unrecognized, early burnout signal, retention lag on frontline
- **IT Services / Consulting:** Recognition stuck in Slack, unprovable at QBR, partner/subcontractor gaps
- **Insurance:** Office-by-office inconsistency, producer retention, license-renewal milestones
- **Construction / Manufacturing:** Recognition never reaches the field, crew wins invisible to operations
- **Real Estate / Hospitality:** Office-by-office inconsistency, agent/location-specific wins, seasonal turnover
- **Retail / Logistics:** Shift-level performance, attendance recognition, frontline morale opaque
- **SaaS / Tech:** Remote team engagement, onboarding moments, milestone recognition

*Always tie Email 3's "deeper" reframe to something specific to that job (compliance training, safety streaks, quota milestones, deal closes, license renewals) — never a generic "great job" mechanic.*

## 7. READY-TO-USE PROMPT (paste into Claude when generating a new sequence)

```
Write a 5-email outbound sequence + a mirrored 5-message LinkedIn sequence for
[PERSONA/TITLE], [VERTICAL/COMPANY_SIZE], selling [PRODUCT: Empuls/Plum/Compass/Loyalife].

Voice rules:
- Sound like a curious human peer, not a vendor. No brochure language, no buzzwords,
  no exclamation points, no em dashes.
- Short sentences, contractions, occasional fragments for punch.
- One proof point per email: a named customer + one concrete detail (timeframe, %,
  headcount reduction, cost saving, or G2 rating 4.6/5, 100+ reports).
- Ground every pain point in a specific, sensory scene from this persona's day-to-day
  (not "employees" or "teams" — the actual role: teller, night nurse, foreman, producer, rep, etc.)

Sequence arc (apply to both channels):
1. Name a narrow, specific pain for this persona/vertical. No pitch. End on a soft,
   low-stakes question.
2. "Following up on X." Introduce [PRODUCT] inline as an aside. First named-customer
   proof point. End with an easy, low-friction CTA.
3. Reframe [PRODUCT] around what this specific role/level cares about (their KPI, their
   pain point, their goal). Second proof point or mechanic. End on an either/or
   diagnostic question.
4. The big-stakes beat — board optics / G2 rating / time-saved / cost-saved, whichever
   fits this seniority. Ask for 15 minutes, framed as "I'd rather show you X than keep
   describing it."
5. The breakup email. No new pitch. Give them a clean, guilt-free exit, door left
   open ("just reply, easy to pick back up").

LinkedIn messages must be shorter and chattier than the emails, and must not repeat
any line, stat, or phrase from the email track word-for-word.

Vary sentence openers across the sequence — don't start every message with "Hi
{{first_name}}," followed by the same structure.
```

## 8. QUICK QC CHECKLIST BEFORE SENDING A DRAFT

- [ ] Could this be read out loud on a cold call without sounding scripted?
- [ ] Is there exactly one named customer proof point in this email — not zero, not three?
- [ ] Is the pain described in a scene (who, when, what specifically), not an abstraction?
- [ ] Does the CTA feel like a favor or an easy yes, not a demo request?
- [ ] Any buzzwords, em dashes, or exclamation points snuck in? Cut them.
- [ ] Does Email 5 actually leave the door open instead of guilt-tripping?
- [ ] Does the LinkedIn track avoid repeating email lines verbatim?
- [ ] Would a 5-minute cold call with this prospect use any of these lines exactly? If not, it's too polished.

## 9. APPLYING THIS GUIDE

**When to use:**
- Generating new persona sequences for any Xoxoday product
- A/B testing subject lines and openers
- Reviewing existing copy for tone consistency
- Training new SDRs or content builders on voice

**How to use:**
1. Pick your product (Empuls/Plum/Compass/Loyalife)
2. Identify your persona (title, vertical, company size)
3. Read the product-specific overlay (#4) and persona-level framing (#5)
4. Use the ready-to-use prompt (#7) as your system instruction
5. Run your draft through the QC checklist (#8) before sending
